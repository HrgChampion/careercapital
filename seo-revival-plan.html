The July 12 cliff, and how to climb back

Site-level demotion Recoverable — no hard penalty

Organic impressions fell 94% overnight on 12 July and never came back. The site is technically healthy — this is a trust problem, not a crawl problem. The most likely trigger is Google's July 2026 core update landing on a site that was carrying 210 fabricated FAQ answers across 58% of its pages.

The four re-test days matter more than the cliff itself. On 30–31 July and 1 August the site served 1,233 impressions and 20 real clicks at 1.1–2.0% CTR — a higher click-through rate than its pre-cliff baseline — then vanished again on 2 August with no deploy in between. Google is periodically re-sampling the domain and deciding against it. That is an algorithmic confidence state, not a manual ban, and confidence states reverse.

Sequence

What actually happened

8 JUL — commit 211b3a4

14 new templated pages shipped

A keyword-driven batch goes live: mba-scholarships, no-gmat-mba-roi, free-mba-programs, part-time-mba-roi and ten more. Thirteen of them have never received a single impression since. They were published onto a domain with no authority budget to spend.

9 JUL

Google's July 2026 core update completes

Google also confirms that smaller core updates now roll continuously without announcement.

11–12 JUL — the cliff

SERP volatility peaks; the site drops out

Third-party volatility trackers hit 10.38 on 11 July and 9.83 on 12 July — among the highest readings of the year. Impressions go 397 → 85 → 17. Average position drifts from ~14 to ~28 and keeps sliding to 60+.

The same-day commit d553bbc "mobile performance improvemnet" is not the cause — it changes link underlines, text colours, a background gradient, the AdSense script strategy and a browserslist entry. Nothing indexable.

30 JUL – 1 AUG

Three normal days, then gone again

Full pre-cliff volume returns with above-baseline CTR, then collapses on 2 August. No deployment occurred on 1 or 2 August. This rules out a code regression and confirms a server-side confidence toggle.

10 AUG

A fourth, smaller re-test

111 impressions at average position 11.8 — the best positional reading in the entire post-cliff window — then back to single digits.

Diagnosis

Root cause

A core update is the trigger, not the cause. Core updates re-score sites that were already sitting on thin ice. These are the things that put this domain on thin ice — in order of how much they matter.

Critical · Google policy violation · verified live

210 FAQ answers that exist only in the markup

49 of 84 live URLs emit FAQPage structured data whose questions and answers appear nowhere on the rendered page. /average-mba-salary declares 11 Q&As and shows none of them. /mba-roi-healthcare, /mba-cost, /mba-worth-it, /executive-mba-roi and roughly thirty others each declare five.

Google's structured data policy is explicit: mark up only content visible to users. Invisible markup is the textbook trigger for spammy-structured-markup enforcement, and at 58% of the site it is a domain-wide signal rather than a page-level slip. This is the single most likely reason the site lost trust rather than merely lost rankings.

Two pages already do it correctly — /post-mba-salary and /mba-roi-mckinsey render a visible FAQ section matching their schema. That is the pattern to copy.

High · Quality dilution

An index full of pages that never earned a visit

16 of 84 URLs drew zero impressions across the full three months. Twelve more render under 400 visible words. Nineteen mba-roi-* pages target heavily overlapping intent using the same section skeleton — "Why X Has the Strongest MBA ROI" → "X Compensation Breakdown" → "Break-Even Model" → "Which Programs Place into X" → "Calculate Your X ROI" — with identical markup and class names beneath the swapped entity.

The prose itself is genuinely distinct (1–3% shingle overlap), so this is not copy-paste spam. But 86 pages published in five months in keyword-named bursts, against a domain with no link equity, reads as scale without substance.

High · The real ceiling

No external authority, and nothing worth citing

US impressions — 79% of all traffic — averaged position 23.4. That is page three. The site was never competing with Poets&Quants, US News or mba.com; it was sitting below them waiting to be noticed. Five Dataset schema blocks assert first-party MBA salary datasets, yet no page cites a visible source for its figures and no page carries a named author.

This is why on-page work alone will not restore the site. Authority is the binding constraint.

Medium · Cannibalisation

Two URLs fighting for one query

/marketing-mba-salary and /mba-roi-marketing ship the identical title tag: "Marketing MBA Salary 2026: $130K–$155K Brand Management, CMO Track & ROI Data". Both rank poorly (positions 32.7 and 37.3). Twenty-plus other titles exceed 62 characters and are being rewritten by Google.

Before you plan

"Peak" was not good

The three months in this export produced 157 clicks — 1.7 per day — at a 0.66% click-through rate from an average position of 23. The pre-cliff run rate of 315 impressions a day was almost entirely page-three placement that nobody clicked.

Restoring the old numbers is the wrong goal. Getting back to 315 impressions/day at position 23 returns the site to roughly two clicks a day. The plan below is built to fix the reason the site sat on page three in the first place — because that fix and the recovery fix are largely the same work.

The plan

Five phases to a defensible recovery

Sequenced deliberately. Phase 1 must ship before anything else, because every later investment compounds on a domain Google currently distrusts.

Phase 0

Confirm the diagnosis

Day 1 · ~2 hours

Two of these checks can only be done in the Search Console UI, and one of them could change the entire plan.

  1. Search Console → Manual actions and Security issues. If a manual action for spammy structured markup is listed, Phase 1 still applies but must be followed by a reconsideration request — and recovery becomes a review queue rather than an algorithmic wait. Check this before writing any code.
  2. Search Console → Indexing → Pages. Compare the "Indexed" count either side of 12 July, and read the "Crawled – currently not indexed" and "Discovered – currently not indexed" buckets. Expect the 16 zero-impression URLs to appear there.
  3. Performance → compare 10 Jun–11 Jul against 13 Jul–13 Aug, broken out by page, country and device. If every page lost proportionally, it is site-level (as expected). If the loss concentrates in a handful of pages, the diagnosis narrows.
  4. Cross-check GA4 organic sessions across the same dates. If GA4 shows no corresponding drop, the problem is Search Console reporting rather than serving — and the plan changes completely.
Phase 1

Stop the policy violation

Week 1 · non-negotiable

Nothing else moves the needle while 58% of the site is emitting structured data for content that does not exist.

  1. Resolve all 210 invisible Q&As across the 49 affected URLs. Per page, choose one: render the FAQ visibly (preferred where the answers genuinely help the reader), or delete the FAQPage block. Never keep schema the page does not show.
  2. Use the existing compliant pages as the reference implementation — app/post-mba-salary/page.tsx and app/mba-roi-mckinsey/page.tsx already render a visible Frequently Asked Questions section that matches their JSON-LD. Extract that into one shared <FaqSection> component that takes the same array used to build the schema, so the two can never diverge again.
  3. Fix the worst offenders first — /average-mba-salary (11 invisible), /mba-roi-healthcare (6), then the ~30 pages carrying five each.
  4. Audit the remaining schema. The five Dataset blocks claim first-party datasets with no visible sourcing — either cite the underlying employment reports on the page or drop the Dataset type. Keep SoftwareApplication + Offer only on pages that actually contain a working tool.
  5. Validate every changed page in the Rich Results Test and re-request indexing for the top 20 URLs.

Done whenA crawl of all 84 URLs returns zero FAQ questions that are absent from visible text — the same check used to find them.

Phase 2

Cut the index down to what deserves to rank

Weeks 1–2

Shrink from 84 URLs to roughly 50. Every page that cannot win a query is diluting the ones that can.

  • The 16 zero-impression URLs — for each, either 301 into the strongest related page or expand it into something genuinely differentiated. Do not leave them live and empty. The 13 from the 8 July batch are the obvious redirect candidates.
  • The 12 pages under 400 visible words — /career-return-roadmap (114), /mba-myth-buster (139), /career-roi-score (159), /salary-negotiation-simulator (177) and the rest — merge into topic hubs, keeping the tool and losing the standalone URL.
  • Resolve the cannibalisation pair. Pick one of /marketing-mba-salary or /mba-roi-marketing, 301 the other, and give the survivor a unique title.
  • Consolidate the 19 mba-roi-* pages into a smaller set of genuinely distinct pages plus one strong parent hub. Firm-level pages (McKinsey, Goldman) that earn impressions stay; overlapping abstractions (/mba-roi-consulting vs /mba-roi-investment-banking vs /mba-roi-big4-vs-mbb) collapse.
  • Rewrite the 20+ titles over 62 characters so Google stops replacing them.

Done whenEvery remaining URL has earned at least one impression in the trailing 90 days, or is under 30 days old.

Phase 4

Build the authority that lifts the ceiling

Weeks 3–12 · runs in parallel

This is the binding constraint. Without referring domains the site returns to page three even after a perfect cleanup.

  • Ship the embeddable calculator widget. A previous session already built /embed/mba-roi-calculator and the /mba-roi-widget landing page with a copy-paste iframe snippet carrying an attribution backlink — verify it survived and deploy it. Every third-party embed is a referring domain plus referral traffic.
  • Turn the data into something citable. An annual MBA ROI Report with original NPV/IRR analysis across schools is genuinely linkable — pitch it to Poets&Quants, BusinessBecause, Fortune Education, Clear Admit and r/MBA. This is the one asset the site has that competitors do not.
  • Respond to journalist requests (Qwoted, Featured, SourceBottle) on MBA financing, salary benchmarks and return-to-work economics. Two or three placements in tier-one outlets outweighs fifty directory links.
  • Build the brand entity — consistent Organization schema, a real LinkedIn presence, and a Wikidata/Crunchbase footprint so Google can resolve CareerReturns as a known publisher.

Target15–25 new referring domains within 90 days, none of them paid or directory-tier.

Phase 5

Wait properly, and measure the right things

Ongoing

Algorithmic trust states are re-evaluated on Google's schedule, not yours. Set expectations accordingly.

  • Expect two distinct recovery signals. Schema and consolidation fixes surface within 2–4 weeks as pages are re-crawled. Site-level trust recovery typically requires a full core update cycle to process the cleaned domain — realistically 6–12 weeks, possibly longer.
  • Freeze new page publishing until Phase 2 is complete. Publishing more pages onto a distrusted domain is the exact behaviour that created the exposure. The 8 July batch is the cautionary example.
  • Watch the re-test days as the leading indicator. If 30 July–1 August style days become more frequent and last longer before Phase 4 lands, the Phase 1 cleanup is working.
  • Export Search Console weekly and track: indexed page count, impressions on the eight priority pages, average position on those pages, referring domains, and days-with-clicks. Not total impressions — that metric misled the last diagnosis.

90-day definition of success25+ clicks/day, CTR above 2%, average position under 12 on the priority set, 50 lean URLs, 20 referring domains. Note that this is materially better than the pre-cliff "peak" of 1.7 clicks/day.
